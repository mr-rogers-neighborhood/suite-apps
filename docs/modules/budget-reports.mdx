title: Budget reports
summary: "The ledger-backed series behind every report tab and dashboard widget."
---

The reports module turns a snapshot and a list of months into chart-ready
series. It owns the month range, budget-vs-actual rows, income and expense,
cash flow, net worth, spending and age of money. It does not own the chart
components, the selected tab or the dashboard records; those live in the
renderer. Every function is pure and rebuilds from the ledger, so a report total
cannot outlive the data it came from.

The shared rule is no stored totals: each report series rebuilds from the ledger. The tests
use a three-month budget with two income months, a refund, one uncategorized
row, an internal transfer and a transfer out of the budget, which is enough to
pin the distinctions the reports make: a refund reduces net spending, an
internal transfer does not change on-budget cash, and an off-budget loan still
counts toward net worth by default.

## legacy/budget/packages/core/src/reports/range.ts#monthsForRange

A fixed range ends at the current month, even when the ledger has no rows for
those months; `ytd` starts at January, and `all` starts at the ledger's first
month but still stops at the current month. That clamp keeps a future-dated
transaction from widening a chart. The month list is the only input the other
series take, so the range decision lives here once.

## legacy/budget/packages/core/src/reports/budgetVsActual.ts#budgetVsActual

The report is a projection over `monthState`, flattened one row per category
with the group name carried alongside. Assigned, activity and available are the
same numbers the plan view shows because they come from the same fold; the
function stores nothing. A category with no activity still appears, which is
what makes the plan and the report comparable line by line.

## legacy/budget/packages/core/src/reports/incomeExpense.ts#incomeExpenseSeries

Income is the ledger's Ready to Assign inflow for the month. Expense is
categorized outflow net of refunds, plus the negative part of uncategorized
activity; a positive uncategorized amount does not become income. Net is income
minus expense. The series follows the supplied months, so a gap in the ledger
becomes a zero-filled point rather than a missing column.

## legacy/budget/packages/core/src/reports/spending.ts#spendingByCategory

Spending is net per category across the whole range, so a refund reduces the row
and a category whose refunds outweigh its spending disappears. Rows are sorted
by spend descending and carry the group name for display. Only categories
present in the snapshot are considered; an uncategorized row has no category to
rank and is left to the income/expense series.

## legacy/budget/packages/core/src/reports/netWorth.ts#netWorthSeries

Net worth is the sum of every account balance at the end of each month.
Off-budget accounts are included by default because a loan is still a liability;
pass `includeOffBudget: false` to see only the budget's own accounts. A month
with no activity still reports the carried balance, so the line is continuous
rather than dropping to zero.

## legacy/budget/packages/core/src/reports/netWorth.ts#accountBalanceSeries

This is the single-account version of net worth, used when a chart follows one
account. It reads the same per-month balance map and returns zero for a month
before the account existed, which is a display choice rather than a claim that
the account had no value. It does not apply the net-worth off-budget filter; the
caller asked for one account.

## legacy/budget/packages/core/src/reports/cashFlow.ts#cashFlowSeries

Cash flow pairs the on-budget balance at each month end with that month's income
and expense. The balance is the ledger's on-budget total, so internal transfers
between on-budget accounts do not change it; the flows come from the
income/expense series so the two charts cannot disagree. The latest point is the
number the summary card shows.

## legacy/budget/packages/core/src/reports/ageOfMoney.ts#ageOfMoney

Age of money is a FIFO lens: income to Ready to Assign creates lots dated by the
transaction, and each outflow consumes the oldest dollars first and is aged by
the weighted average of the lots it used. Internal transfers and
on-budget-to-on-budget moves are skipped, so only money entering and leaving the
budget counts. A month with no outflow is `null`, and the headline average
covers the last ten outflows; it is descriptive, not a score.
