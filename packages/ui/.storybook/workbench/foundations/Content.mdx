# Content

The system owns the words a person sees when the product is working, waiting or refusing. A pack
owns its domain nouns; it does not invent a second voice for buttons, errors or empty states.

## Voice

- **Plain.** Say the thing. “Sync is off” beats “Synchronisation has not been initialised”.
- **Specific.** Name the object and the action. “Delete this Screen?” not “Are you sure?”.
- **Calm.** A failure is a fact and a next step, not an apology and not blame.
- **Sentence case.** Buttons, labels, headings and menu items. Product names keep their spelling.
- **No architecture.** “Pack”, “document” and “relay” are developer words unless the person is
  already in a developer surface; use “app”, “file” and “sync”.

## Buttons

- Start with a verb: **Create Screen**, **Start sync**, **Remove pack**.
- A destructive action names its object: **Delete “Week 48”**, not **Delete**.
- One primary action per surface. Secondary actions are quiet; a third action belongs in a menu.

## Errors

Say what happened, why if it is knowable, and what to do next. Keep the code out of the sentence.

```text
Good:  The relay stopped after three restarts. Start it again, or open Settings → Sync.
Bad:   Error 0x80004005.
```

Do not promise a retry that the product does not perform.

## Empty states

An empty state says what belongs here and offers one action. It does not explain the architecture.

```text
Good:  No Screens yet. Create one to group the apps you use together.
Bad:   The Screen document has no widget placements.
```

## Destructive actions

Name the thing, say what is removed, and keep the confirmation button specific. Undo is better than
a confirmation when the operation is reversible.

## Where this shows up

- `Workbench/Patterns/New tab` has the picker's copy.
- A pack's launcher, dialogs and empty states use the same rules.
- The host owns the words for chrome; a pack owns the words for its content.
